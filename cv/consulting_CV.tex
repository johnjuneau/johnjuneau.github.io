{\CronosLt\fontsize{12pt}{13pt}\selectfont\textsc{Job Market Paper}}\par
\vspace{3pt}
\researchHeading
  {\href{https://johnjuneau.github.io/files/of-fathers-and-farms.pdf}{Of Fathers and Farms: How Intergenerational Persistence Shapes Structural Transformation}}
  {} 
  {}
Uses linked father--son records from historical U.S. Census microdata and a model of structural change to estimate how inherited skills and preferences shape sector choice across generations. The model accounts for endogenous selection and estimates these mechanisms from observed sector choices and earnings distributions. Counterfactual simulations imply that intergenerational persistence slowed U.S. industrialization from 1880 to 1940 by 23.2 percent, almost entirely through inherited skills; omitting persistence also substantially overstates the relative nonagricultural technology growth needed to explain the transition.

\researchHeading
  {\href{https://johnjuneau.github.io/files/state-tax-cuts.pdf}{The Stimulative Effect of State Tax Cuts Across the Income Distribution}}
  {with Byron Lutz}
  {}
Uses thirty years of detailed income-tax-return microdata and multiple panel-data research designs, including event-study local projections, to estimate how the distributional targeting of state income-tax changes affects employment. Finds positive employment responses to broadly targeted tax cuts, with no corresponding evidence for cuts targeted at the highest-income households.
